## Appendix: Methodology

**Research approach**

Data compiled from publicly available sources current as of August 2026. Primary sources included official center and company websites, leadership/founder pages, and institutional or corporate announcements. Secondary sources included DOE/NSF funding notices, state economic-development releases, university press offices, SEC filings (for public companies), and established trackers (Quantum Computing Report, Dealroom, Entangled Future / Quantum Navigator-style directories, Seedtable, etc.). Founding years, leadership, focus areas, and locations were cross-checked against multiple sources. Funding figures reflect only publicly disclosed amounts and are approximate (often cumulative or multi-year).

**Categorization scheme used**

- **Primary**: Institutional type (University Research Institutes/Centers; Regional/Multi-Institution Consortia; DOE National QIS Research Centers; Startups/Commercial Companies; International Anchors).
- **Secondary**: Geography / innovation hub (shown in Location column).
- **Tertiary**: Technical focus / platform (full-stack, trapped-ion, superconducting, neutral-atom, photonic, silicon, networking, sensing, theory/algorithms, materials, software/ecosystem).
- History: Founding year added for every entry to show timeline.
- Additional tags available for future tracking: funding profile, public/private status, maturity, open-access facilities.

**Scope and limitations**

Emphasized named, trackable centers and the most visible pure-play or major quantum startups rather than every research group or small software-only firm. Big Tech internal efforts (IBM Quantum, Google Quantum AI, etc.) are noted in context but not listed as startups. Leadership, exact funding, and valuations change frequently; this is a living snapshot intended for ongoing updates.
